Go-to-market plan

Selling seventeen garden homes with Angela Webb

The Mantra sells a feeling before a floor plan: stillness, good neighbours and a garden at the door. The campaign builds a waiting list of qualified buyers who see plans and pricing privately before public release, with a natural first audience in Christchurch's meditation and wellbeing community. The concept is not yet consented, so every piece is labelled as an artist's impression and concept design. Figures marked "estimate" are starting assumptions to test.

Perfect customer

Primary

Calm-seeking downsizers

Owners aged 55 to 78 in larger homes in St Albans, Merivale, Mairehau, Richmond and Papanui, often long-time meditators or yoga practitioners, with equity and grown children.

  • Trigger: the house and garden are too much, but they still want a garden and people around them.
  • Wants: quiet, sun, a small productive garden, neighbours who share their values, close to town.
  • Objections: stairs in three-level homes (answered by the two-level homes and the Eastern House), buying off the plans before consent.

Primary

Wellbeing-minded professionals

Singles and couples aged 30 to 55 who meditate, practise yoga or follow a slow-living lifestyle, working in the central city or from home.

  • Trigger: leaving a rental, wanting a first home with a community, or moving back to Christchurch.
  • Wants: a two-bedroom townhouse, bike storage, EV charging, a quiet place to sit.
  • Objections: price per square metre and timing.

Secondary

Young families

Families with one or two children who want the three-bedroom homes around the commons, a car-free path and a safe shared garden.

  • Objections: school zones and space; answered with the commons, garden and nearby schools.

Secondary

Meditation community networks

People connected through local meditation centres, retreats and wellbeing groups, who may buy together as friends and neighbours.

  • Objections: wanting to buy near friends; answered with group previews and first choice of neighbouring homes.

Tracking

  • Meta Pixel and Conversions API: a "Lead" event on successful registration, browser and server, deduplicated.
  • Google Ads conversion tag and GA4: a "generate_lead" event with home type and budget band as parameters.
  • UTM tags on every ad, so each registration shows its platform, angle and ad number.
  • Weekly report: spend, registrations, cost per registration, budget bands and the best ads.

Traditional media: billboards, print and outdoor

For seventeen homes the traditional mix should be warm and local: one or two outdoor sites on the routes north-east buyers use, a few print insertions, community noticeboards and beautiful site hoarding on Whitmore Street. Every piece carries Angela, one call to action and the site address. The mock-ups below are flat artwork concepts in simple settings for discussion, not booked placements.

Booking a Christchurch billboard

Step 1Brief suppliers

Ask the main outdoor companies (such as JCDecaux, oOh!media and LUMO Digital Outdoor) for premium sites and rates.

Step 2Choose sites

Routes buyers use daily: Bealey Avenue, Papanui Road, Fitzgerald Avenue, Cranford Street and the central city.

Step 3Book dates

Digital boards book in 1 to 2 week slots; printed boards usually 4 weeks or more.

Step 4Supply artwork

The supplier's exact size. Seven words or fewer, plus Angela and the web address.

Step 5Track

Watch direct visits and searches for "The Mantra Christchurch" during the run.

Rules for every ad

  • Label renders as artist's impressions and plans as concept designs subject to consent, in line with the Fair Trading Act.
  • No prices, completion dates or wellbeing claims that can't be backed. Lifestyle images of people are illustrative and say so.
  • Include Bayleys branding and the licence line wherever the format has room.
